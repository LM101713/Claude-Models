## Return budget for the forward-blocking pawl (the only one that needs the rack to re-centre)

- Spring arm at full lift: 0.9 N. Through a 41° cam that is about 0.7 N on the rack (falling to zero as the tooth seats).
- Rack detent (finger on the shuttle, relaxed when seated): design value 3 N of centring force.
- Available to re-centre the rack: about 3.7 N at the start of the return, 3 N at the end.
- Drag allowed at the rack with a factor of two in hand: about 1.5 N at the end of the return. That is 0.74 N at the wheel rim.
- This is why the wheel must turn freely and why the detent acts on the rack, not on the wheel.

## Shock

- Rack about 4.6 g; wheel about 15 g, which the rack feels as about 23 g.
- The detent holds the rack up to about 67 g of deceleration along the tool. A drop onto a hard floor is typically several hundred g or more.
- So a drop on the nose or tail can shift the rack and lift the pawl when the carry bolt is not engaged. With the carry bolt engaged the rack cannot move.

## Carry bolt and tail cap

- Wheel forced against the bolt: about 60 N at the rack from a hard thumb push. Bolt shear (two planes) 1.5 MPa; bearing in the rack notch 1.8 MPa.
- If the pawl has failed and only the bolt holds: the carriage can still move 2.5 mm plus backlash. Parked tip stays 0.7 mm inside the mouth.
- Tail rails at 100 N along the tool: 0.7 MPa on two 4 x 18 mm necks, in the layer plane.
- Tail rails at 200 N along the tool: 1.4 MPa on two 4 x 18 mm necks, in the layer plane.
